First time here

Light level is how bright you want the screen, 1 to 100. Below about 10 it goes further down than the system slider can.

Warmth is colour temperature. 1700K is candlelight; stock Android stops at 2596K.

Flicker-safe mode is for eye strain. Most OLED panels strobe at low brightness; this keeps the backlight up and takes the darkness out of the colour instead, so there is nothing to strobe.

Nothing here is permanent. Reset to stock at the bottom of this tab puts everything back, and removing the module undoes it entirely.

If the screen is ever too dark to find that button, press and hold anywhere on this page for two seconds and it resets. That is the way back when you cannot see what you are aiming at.

Flicker-safe mode

Holds the backlight above the point where your panel starts strobing, and takes the rest of the dimming out of the colour matrix instead. If low brightness gives you eye strain or headaches, this is the setting that helps. Costs a little contrast in dark scenes.
PWM knee—
Lower this until flicker stops being visible. The default is a guess, not a measurement — every panel is different.

Device

Re-probe re-checks what this device supports — Night Light, Extra Dim, the panel node, and whether the overlay is actually in effect. It changes nothing; it only refreshes what is shown above.
Reset to stock clears warmth, dimming and flicker-safe mode and puts brightness back to something readable. Hold the button for a second — a screen you cannot read is a screen you cannot use to fix itself, so this one should not be reachable by a mis-tap.
If it ever goes too dark to read: hold that button, or from a computer run adb shell su -c "lunectl reset". Removing the module restores stock display settings on its way out, and holding Volume Down during boot disables every module.

Labs › Screen & rendering is where the app hands warmth and dimming to this module instead of drawing a matte over your screen. The switch only appears there once the module is installed, which it now is.

Quiet hours

Windownot scheduled
Custom to
A preset applies the moment you tap it. The custom pickers do nothing until you tap Set. Either way you are writing one thing: the window. If that window contains the current time, quiet mode starts immediately, which is the point of a schedule rather than a bug.
Use Do Not Disturb
Quiet hours do two independent things, and this switch controls the second:

1. Apply the levers to the apps you listed below. Does nothing if that list is empty.
2. Turn on Do Not Disturb for the whole phone — which is why setting a window quiets everything even with no apps chosen. Turn this off if you only want the per-app levers.

Do Not Disturb is set to priority, not total silence, so alarms still go off. Windows that cross midnight work as you expect.
Quiet mode right now

Quieted apps

Default levers: no wake locks, may not turn the screen on, may not take the screen over. Tap levers to cycle which apply.

Allowed through

These may break through Do Not Disturb during quiet hours. Put the people who matter here — a partner, a parent, your on-call app.

Re-engagement watcher

Snoozes notifications matching the pattern list — "we miss you", "your streak is about to expire" — but only for apps you added above. It polls, so matching notifications flash briefly before they go, and it costs some battery. Removing them before they are posted would need framework hooks, which this module deliberately does not use.

Today

Re-probe re-checks what this Android version allows — which levers exist, whether notifications are reachable. Nothing changes on your device; it only refreshes what is shown above. Worth a tap after a system update.

Release everything hands every quieted app its permissions back, clears the allow list and the schedule, and turns Do Not Disturb off. Your apps behave normally again immediately. Stats are kept; nothing else survives.

About

Lune Bridge by uraniam9 · GPL-3.0

Built as the root companion to SonoLune, a calm-first sleep and focus app. SonoLune works without root; with this module installed it stops drawing a matte over your screen and hands warmth and dimming to the display pipeline instead.

Something broken, or something missing? These open a form on GitHub that already asks the right questions, so a report does not turn into five rounds of me asking which device you are on.
No warranty. This is a root module: it changes display settings and app permissions on your device, and you use it at your own risk. It is provided as-is with no warranty of any kind, and the author is not liable for any damage or data loss.

In practice it is conservative by design — it only moves limits Android already enforces, never writes to a hardware node it does not recognise, lifts every permission it set when uninstalled, and stands down on its own if a boot fails. But nobody can test every device, and yours might be the one that behaves differently.
